01

The trail starts at the house

Walk out the front door, cross the street, and you're in the FINS trailhead. No driving anywhere first.

Red: the home on W Verdin. Purple: the FINS trailhead across the street, dozens of logged runs fanning into the desert.
Two ways out. North (blue): the wash spine, two flat miles to the lakes. South/east (clay): straight into FINS singletrack, then open desert toward the Regional Park.

The path goes two ways from the house. North, the wash spine runs two flat miles to the lakes, easy enough for anyone, kids and grandparents included. South and east, the singletrack starts across the street and climbs toward the mountains for the people who want a real ride or hike.

That eastern stretch keeps going, all the way out toward the 19,840-acre Estrella Mountain Regional Park. So when you come visit, there's a lazy lake stroll out one direction and a proper desert adventure out the other. Same front door.

Four outings on foot, from a singletrack sampler to a real desert effort. Round-trip from the front door.

04

Roll out the door and keep going

From the front door: drop into singletrack a reviewer calls "Disneyland for your mountain bike," spin north for glassy lake pavement, then point east into open desert until the mountains swallow you. Five rides, each bigger than the last.

The warm-up · paved · easy

The Lake Spin

Glass-smooth pavement around both lakes, flat and fast. Coffee at the marketplace, a lap with the kids, a sunrise easy-day. Two easy miles up the spine to the water.

From the door · any bike, any age
The playground · across the street

FINS Singletrack

Fantasy Island Northern Singletrack: berms, flow, technical features, structures. One rider's verdict, "like Disneyland for your mountain bike." 4.7 stars. Roll out of the driveway, then hours of riding.

Beginner flow to expert lines
The skills lab · free

Foothills Bike Park

Public pump track and jump lines, beginner through advanced. Sharpen handling, let the kids session for hours. Sits on the eastern trail's doorstep, so it doubles as the launch pad.

Minutes away · free · all levels
The main event · ~7 mi · the east arm

Estrella Foothills Loop

Past the Bike Park, then east into raw Sonoran desert: "desert riding at its finest." Climbs exposed faces to bench-spots over the whole valley, past the landmark riders call the Circle of Trust, finishing with 3-foot+ jumps if you've got the speed. Forks everywhere, so it's a route you learn and own.

Moderate–technical · ride it tubeless
The expedition · all day · open-ended

Into the Backcountry

Where the Foothills Loop forks deeper, the desert opens into a choose-your-distance day stretching toward the 19,840-acre Estrella Mountain Regional Park and its 33 miles of trail. Unsigned, exposed, real.

From the door · expert · bring everything

The deep desert routes are informal, unmarked, and fork constantly. Load a GPS track (MTB Project and Trailforks both map this network), carry more water than feels reasonable, and ride the big stuff October through April.

It's the desert. Plan around the sun.

Summer is hot, and our first July week proves it: highs of 103 to 110°F under an excessive heat warning. Get out at dawn. October through May is the prime season.

Summer: dawn or skip it. Mid-80s before sunrise are runnable; by 9 AM it's done. Carry more water than you think, the desert trails have almost no shade.
